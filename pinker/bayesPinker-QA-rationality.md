Further readings

* **Statistical Rethinking: A Bayesian Course with Examples in R and Stan** by Richard McElreath. Authored by an evolutionary anthropologist, this text is widely considered the definitive guide for applying Bayesian inference to complex biological and social systems. It builds generalized linear multilevel models from first principles, emphasizing causal inference, maximum entropy, and structural logic. The computational approach relies on native R code that integrates seamlessly into script-based data analysis environments, ensuring the reader understands the mechanics by performing calculations step-by-step rather than relying on black-box algorithms.

* **Bayesian Models: A Statistical Primer for Ecologists** by N. Thompson Hobbs and Mevin B. Hooten. This text explicitly bridges the gap between abstract statistical theory and biological realism. Rather than focusing on software-specific syntax, it utilizes rigorous mathematical notation and directed acyclic graphs (DAGs) to construct hierarchical models for ecological, spatial, and biogeographical data. This structural focus makes the underlying logic highly adaptable for researchers writing custom analytical workflows in Python, Bash, or Perl.

* **Doing Bayesian Data Analysis: A Tutorial Introduction with R and JAGS/Stan** by John K. Kruschke. This provides a highly accessible, hands-on transition from frequentist statistics to Bayesian parameter estimation. It demystifies the mechanics of generating a posterior distribution by comparing both statistical paradigms side-by-side using identical models. The scripts are fully transparent, allowing for straightforward incorporation into plain-text editing environments.

* **Probability Theory: The Logic of Science** by E. T. Jaynes. Moving beyond applied computational modeling, this foundational treatise frames Bayesian probability as the quantitative extension of formal logic into domains of uncertainty. It rigorously defends the objective assignment of prior probabilities and serves as essential reading for defining the epistemological and bioethical boundaries of scientific reasoning.
